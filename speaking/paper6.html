Speaking — Practice Paper 6

50 marks · 13 minutes total · 4 parts · Topic: Culture & Society

How to use this page: Practise with a partner (one plays the interlocutor, one the candidate), or record yourself speaking. Click any question to highlight it. Aim for natural, fluent responses — not memorised scripts.
Part 1 Personal Questions 3 minutes
3 min The interlocutor asks your name, spelling, country of origin, then chooses up to 5 questions — one from each topic below.

The interlocutor starts: "Hello. My name's [name]. Can you spell your surname for me, please?" then "Where are you from?" — then selects one question from each topic.

Music
  • ▸What kind of music do you listen to?
  • ▸Can you play a musical instrument?
  • ▸How important is music in your life?
Q: What kind of music do you listen to?

"My tastes are fairly varied, depending on my mood. I listen to a lot of indie and acoustic music when I want to focus, and something more upbeat when I'm exercising. What I find remarkable is the effect music has on your state of mind — it can lift you when you're tired or completely change the atmosphere of a space. I also love live music; there's something about a live performance that recordings simply cannot replicate."
Reading
  • ▸How often do you read for pleasure?
  • ▸What kinds of books do you prefer?
  • ▸Do you think reading is becoming less popular? Why?
Q: How often do you read for pleasure?

"I try to read every day — even twenty minutes before bed makes a difference. I personally prefer literary fiction, though I read non-fiction too when a topic captures me. What I've noticed is that reading regularly improves your writing and communication skills almost without you realising it — you absorb vocabulary and rhythm through exposure rather than effort."
Cinema & Film
  • ▸How often do you go to the cinema?
  • ▸What is your favourite type of film?
  • ▸Has a film ever made you think differently about something?
Q: How often do you go to the cinema?

"About once a month, I'd say — though I'd like it to be more often. I think there's something irreplaceable about watching a film on the big screen — the sound, the scale, and particularly the shared experience when an entire audience reacts to something simultaneously. I tend to prefer thought-provoking films over purely entertaining ones, though I don't have anything against a good comedy."
Family
  • ▸How important is family to you?
  • ▸How do you keep in touch with family members who live far away?
  • ▸How do you think family life has changed in recent decades?
Q: How important is family to you?

"Extremely important. I think the foundation of trust and unconditional support that family provides is very difficult to find elsewhere. That said, I also think family relationships take real effort — you have to invest time to keep those connections strong, especially as people get busier and sometimes live further apart. I'm lucky that my family is quite close and we stay in regular contact."
Celebrations
  • ▸What is your favourite celebration or festival?
  • ▸How do you usually celebrate your birthday?
  • ▸Are there any traditions in your family that are important to you?
Q: What is your favourite celebration or festival?

"I'd say New Year — there's a warmth and sense of shared tradition about it that I really value. What I love most is that it brings together people who might not see each other at other points in the year. The food, the rituals, the sense of marking a moment in time — it all feels genuinely meaningful rather than just routine."
Part 2 Role-plays 3 minutes
3 min Two role-play scenarios. In Scenario 1 Candidate A initiates; in Scenario 2 Candidate B initiates.
Scenario 1 — A initiates
Surprise birthday party
Candidate A: you are organising the party and need help deciding the venue and activities.
Candidate B: you are helping to plan it.
"I want to make this birthday really special — I was thinking we could do something a bit different this year."
Scenario 2 — B initiates
B starts Community arts festival
Candidate B: you are a local artist who wants to participate in the festival.
Candidate A: you are the festival organiser.
"I heard you're organising the arts festival — I'd really love to get involved."
Part 3 Discussion 4 minutes
4 min Discuss the points on your task sheet together and reach decisions. You have 20 seconds to read before starting.

Task sheet:

How should our city celebrate its cultural heritage?
Situation: Your city is planning a new cultural project. Discuss the options below and decide which would best celebrate and share the city's cultural heritage.
▸Building a new museum
▸Organising an annual street festival
▸Creating a public art trail
▸Offering free cultural events for young people
Useful language: "I think we should…" · "What about…?" · "I'd prefer… because…" · "Could we compromise on…?" · "That's a good point, but…" · "Let's decide on…"
Part 4 Monologue 3 minutes
3 min 30 seconds to make notes → speak for up to 2 minutes → follow-up questions from interlocutor.

The interlocutor gives you one of the topic cards below. You have 30 seconds to prepare, then speak for up to 2 minutes. The interlocutor will then ask follow-up questions.

Card 1
The role of art and culture in society
Follow-up questions
  • Do you think art is important? Why?
  • Should governments fund art and culture?
  • What kind of art do you find most interesting?

Art and culture play a fundamental role in shaping who we are as individuals and as societies. At the most basic level, they give us a shared vocabulary of meaning — through music, literature, film, and visual art, communities develop a sense of identity and collective memory.

First of all, art challenges us. Great works of literature or cinema force us to inhabit perspectives different from our own, developing empathy and critical thinking in ways that straightforward information cannot. A novel about poverty can change attitudes more effectively than any statistic.

Secondly, culture has significant economic value. The creative industries generate enormous revenues globally and attract tourism, investment, and talent to cities. But I think the deeper value is harder to quantify — art gives people a reason to feel proud of where they come from, and it provides meaning during difficult times. Think of how music sustained people during periods of hardship throughout history. To sum up, I believe a society without investment in art and culture is impoverished in ways that go far beyond the economic — it loses its soul.

Card 2
Social media and modern communication
Follow-up questions
  • How has social media changed the way people communicate?
  • What do you think are the benefits and drawbacks?
  • Do you think people communicate better now than in the past?

Social media has transformed human communication in ways both remarkable and deeply concerning. On the positive side, it has connected people across continents, given voice to communities that were previously ignored, and enabled social movements to organise with unprecedented speed.

However, I think the negative consequences deserve serious attention. First of all, the business model of most social media platforms is built on engagement, and the content that generates most engagement is often outrage, fear, or division. This has had a measurable effect on political polarisation and mental health, particularly among young people.

Another important point is the impact on attention. When we are constantly moving between short pieces of content, we lose the ability to sustain focus on complex problems — and complex problems are exactly what our world needs us to solve. That said, I do not think the answer is to abandon social media, but to use it more intentionally and to hold platforms accountable for the effects their algorithms create. To sum up, social media is a powerful tool — like all powerful tools, its value depends entirely on how wisely it is used.

Monologue tips: Organise into: introduction → 2–3 main points → conclusion. Use connectors: "First of all…", "Another important point is…", "To sum up…". Aim for 2 full minutes — don't rush. Examiners reward range and fluency, not perfection.
