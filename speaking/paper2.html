Speaking — Practice Paper 2

50 marks · 13 minutes total · 4 parts · Topic: Work & Education

How to use this page: Practise with a partner (one plays the interlocutor, one the candidate), or record yourself speaking. Click any question to highlight it. Aim for natural, fluent responses — not memorised scripts.
Part 1 Personal Questions 3 minutes
3 min The interlocutor asks your name, spelling, country of origin, then chooses up to 5 questions — one from each topic below.

The interlocutor starts: "Hello. My name's [name]. Can you spell your surname for me, please?" then "Where are you from?" — then selects one question from each topic.

Work
  • ▸Do you have a job at the moment?
  • ▸What kind of work would you like to do in the future?
  • ▸What do you think makes a good workplace?
Q: What do you think makes a good workplace?

"For me, a good workplace comes down to two things: clarity and respect. You need to know what's expected of you, and you need to feel that your contribution is valued. I also think the physical environment matters — a space where people feel comfortable is more productive than one driven by pressure. What I've noticed is that the best workplaces tend to trust their employees rather than micromanage them. That trust makes a real difference to motivation."
Education
  • ▸How long have you been studying English?
  • ▸What subjects did you enjoy most at school?
  • ▸Do you think university education is important for everyone?
Q: Do you think university education is important for everyone?

"Personally, I don't think it's essential for everyone — it depends entirely on what someone wants to do. University gives you deep specialist knowledge and certain career doors, but on the other hand many skilled professions value practical training far more. What I believe is that the most important thing is that people find a path that genuinely suits them, rather than going to university simply because it's expected."
Daily Routine
  • ▸What time do you usually wake up?
  • ▸How do you usually spend your evenings?
  • ▸Do you prefer busy or quiet days?
Q: How do you usually spend your evenings?

"Generally I try to wind down after a busy day. I might cook something — I find that quite relaxing, actually — then catch up on news or watch something. What I've found is that having a consistent evening routine makes a real difference to how well I sleep. That said, I do enjoy socialising during the week occasionally, whether that's meeting a friend for dinner or going to a class of some kind."
Hobbies
  • ▸What do you enjoy doing in your free time?
  • ▸Have you taken up any new hobbies recently?
  • ▸Is there a hobby you would like to try?
Q: Have you taken up any new hobbies recently?

"Yes, actually — I started photography about six months ago. I'd always been interested in it but never found the time. What I really enjoy is that it forces you to slow down and really look at the world around you rather than rushing through it. Unexpectedly, I've also met some interesting people through it, which has been a bonus."
Shopping
  • ▸How often do you go shopping?
  • ▸Do you prefer shopping online or in a store?
  • ▸What was the last thing you bought that you were really pleased with?
Q: Do you prefer shopping online or in a store?

"It depends on what I'm buying. For clothes, I much prefer physical shops — I want to try things on and get the fit right. But for books, electronics, or household items, online shopping is simply more convenient. The main downside of online shopping, I find, is that things sometimes look quite different from the photos, so you end up returning a lot. I think a balance of both is ideal."
Part 2 Role-plays 3 minutes
3 min Two role-play scenarios. In Scenario 1 Candidate A initiates; in Scenario 2 Candidate B initiates.
Scenario 1 — A initiates
Job interview
Candidate A: you are the interviewer asking about experience, skills, and career goals.
Candidate B: you are applying for a marketing assistant position.
"Thank you for coming in today. Could you start by telling me a little about yourself?"
Scenario 2 — B initiates
B starts Group project deadline
Candidate B: you are worried the project will not be finished in time.
Candidate A: you think there is enough time but need to plan.
"I'm really stressed about this project — I don't think we have enough time."
Part 3 Discussion 4 minutes
4 min Discuss the points on your task sheet together and reach decisions. You have 20 seconds to read before starting.

Task sheet:

How should our college improve the student experience?
Situation: Your college is planning changes for next year. Discuss the options below and decide which improvements would benefit students most.
▸Longer library opening hours
▸A new sports facility
▸More career guidance sessions
▸A student café with healthier food options
Useful language: "I think we should…" · "What about…?" · "I'd prefer… because…" · "Could we compromise on…?" · "That's a good point, but…" · "Let's decide on…"
Part 4 Monologue 3 minutes
3 min 30 seconds to make notes → speak for up to 2 minutes → follow-up questions from interlocutor.

The interlocutor gives you one of the topic cards below. You have 30 seconds to prepare, then speak for up to 2 minutes. The interlocutor will then ask follow-up questions.

Card 1
The importance of learning a foreign language
Follow-up questions
  • What languages have you studied?
  • How does language learning affect the way you think?
  • What advice would you give to someone starting to learn a new language?

Learning a foreign language is, in my view, one of the most valuable things a person can do. First of all, it opens doors professionally — employers increasingly look for candidates who can communicate across cultures. In my own experience, knowing English has given me access to opportunities that would simply not exist otherwise.

Another important point is the cognitive benefit. Research shows that bilingual people develop stronger problem-solving skills and can even delay the onset of dementia. Beyond that, learning a language forces you to think differently — you start to understand how other cultures organise ideas and express the world around them.

Perhaps most importantly, language learning builds empathy. When you struggle to express yourself in another language, you gain a deeper respect for people who communicate in a second or third language every day. To sum up, I believe every person who has the chance to learn a foreign language should take it — the rewards go far beyond communication.

Card 2
Your ideal job
Follow-up questions
  • What qualities do you think are most important in a job?
  • How important is salary compared to job satisfaction?
  • How do you imagine your working life in ten years?

My ideal job would combine creativity, independence, and the opportunity to make a positive difference. If I could design my perfect career, it would involve working on meaningful projects — perhaps in education, design, or environmental sustainability — where I can see the real-world impact of my work.

First of all, I think flexibility is crucial. I am most productive when I can organise my own schedule and work environment. That said, I also value being part of a team — collaboration sparks ideas that I could never generate alone. So ideally, I would have the autonomy of an individual contributor but with a supportive team around me.

As for salary, while financial security matters, I genuinely believe that job satisfaction is the greater motivator. Studies consistently show that once basic needs are met, additional income does little to increase happiness. What keeps people engaged long-term is a sense of purpose and growth. To sum up, my ideal job would offer meaningful work, flexibility, and human connection — that combination would make getting up in the morning something I actually look forward to.

Monologue tips: Organise into: introduction → 2–3 main points → conclusion. Use connectors: "First of all…", "Another important point is…", "To sum up…". Aim for 2 full minutes — don't rush. Examiners reward range and fluency, not perfection.
