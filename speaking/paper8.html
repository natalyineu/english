Speaking — Practice Paper 8

50 marks · 13 minutes total · 4 parts · Topic: The Modern World

How to use this page: Practise with a partner (one plays the interlocutor, one the candidate), or record yourself speaking. Click any question to highlight it. Aim for natural, fluent responses — not memorised scripts.
Part 1 Personal Questions 3 minutes
3 min The interlocutor asks your name, spelling, country of origin, then chooses up to 5 questions — one from each topic below.

The interlocutor starts: "Hello. My name's [name]. Can you spell your surname for me, please?" then "Where are you from?" — then selects one question from each topic.

Technology
  • ▸How important is technology in your daily life?
  • ▸What do you think has been the most important technological development of the last twenty years?
  • ▸Are there any technologies you find difficult or annoying to use?
Q: How important is technology in your daily life?

"Very important, I'd say — perhaps more than I'd like. I use technology for work, communication, entertainment, and navigation, so it's woven into almost everything I do. What concerns me sometimes is the dependency it creates — I've noticed how uncomfortable I feel without my phone, which I think is worth reflecting on. That said, the benefits are undeniable, and I wouldn't want to go back."
The Future
  • ▸What do you think the world will be like in fifty years?
  • ▸Are you generally optimistic or pessimistic about the future?
  • ▸What do you think will be the biggest challenge facing your generation?
Q: What do you think the world will be like in fifty years?

"I'm cautiously optimistic. I think technology will have transformed daily life significantly — automation will have changed many jobs, and renewable energy will hopefully have replaced most fossil fuels. What I'm less certain about is whether society will have kept pace with these changes — the gap between what technology can do and what regulation and social norms allow tends to create real tension."
Cities
  • ▸Do you live in a city or the countryside? Which do you prefer?
  • ▸What do you think are the advantages of living in a big city?
  • ▸How do you think cities will change in the next few decades?
Q: Do you live in a city or the countryside? Which do you prefer?

"I live in a city, and overall I prefer it — I appreciate the convenience, the variety of things to do, and the energy. But I do find that I need to escape occasionally. What I think makes a city genuinely liveable is good green space — parks and rivers within the urban environment. The ideal for me would be a medium-sized city with easy access to countryside."
Communication
  • ▸How has communication changed during your lifetime?
  • ▸Do you prefer to call people or send messages?
  • ▸Do you think people communicate more or less effectively than they did before the internet?
Q: How has communication changed during your lifetime?

"Dramatically. When I was younger, you'd call someone on a landline or write a letter. Now we're in constant contact through messages, video calls, and social media. The speed and convenience are remarkable — I can speak to someone on the other side of the world as if they're next door. But I think something has been lost too — the thoughtfulness that came with slower communication, where you had to choose your words more carefully."
Money
  • ▸How important is money to you?
  • ▸Do you think you are good at managing money?
  • ▸Do you think it is better to save money or spend it on experiences?
Q: How important is money to you?

"Important enough that I want to live comfortably and not be stressed about basics, but beyond that point, I genuinely don't think more money makes you meaningfully happier — the research on this is quite consistent. What I value more is having time and freedom to do things I find meaningful. I suppose the honest answer is money matters most when you don't have enough of it, and its importance diminishes once your basic needs are secure."
Part 2 Role-plays 3 minutes
3 min Two role-play scenarios. In Scenario 1 Candidate A initiates; in Scenario 2 Candidate B initiates.
Scenario 1 — A initiates
Business loan at a bank
Candidate A: you are the bank manager asking about the business plan and finances.
Candidate B: you want a loan to open a small café.
"Thank you for coming in. I understand you'd like to discuss a business loan?"
Scenario 2 — B initiates
B starts New development nearby
Candidate B: you are unhappy about the new building and its effects on the neighbourhood.
Candidate A: you think the development will bring benefits.
"Have you seen what they're building on the corner? I think it's going to cause real problems."
Part 3 Discussion 4 minutes
4 min Discuss the points on your task sheet together and reach decisions. You have 20 seconds to read before starting.

Task sheet:

How should our town spend a large government grant?
Situation: Your town is being offered funding for one major project. Discuss the options below and decide which would bring the greatest benefit to the community.
▸Building affordable housing
▸Improving public transport
▸Creating new green spaces and parks
▸Developing a new technology and business hub
Useful language: "I think we should…" · "What about…?" · "I'd prefer… because…" · "Could we compromise on…?" · "That's a good point, but…" · "Let's decide on…"
Part 4 Monologue 3 minutes
3 min 30 seconds to make notes → speak for up to 2 minutes → follow-up questions from interlocutor.

The interlocutor gives you one of the topic cards below. You have 30 seconds to prepare, then speak for up to 2 minutes. The interlocutor will then ask follow-up questions.

Card 1
How technology is changing the world of work
Follow-up questions
  • How has technology already changed the type of work people do?
  • Are you concerned that technology might replace human jobs?
  • What skills do you think will be most important in the future workplace?

Technology is transforming the world of work at a pace that is genuinely unprecedented in human history. While previous industrial revolutions automated physical tasks, the current wave of artificial intelligence and automation is beginning to affect cognitive and creative work as well — the jobs we thought were safe.

First of all, the changes create enormous opportunities. Remote work technology has given millions of people flexibility that was previously impossible, allowing them to live where they choose and structure their days around their most productive hours. New industries and entirely new types of jobs are emerging constantly, many of which could not have been imagined a decade ago.

However, the disruption is also real and painful for many workers, particularly those in routine-based occupations that are most vulnerable to automation. I think the key challenge for societies is ensuring that the gains from technological change are distributed fairly — that workers displaced by automation receive the support and retraining they need. To sum up, technology is neither inherently good nor bad for workers — what matters is the policy choices societies make about how to manage this transition and who bears the costs.

Card 2
The benefits and challenges of living in a globalised world
Follow-up questions
  • What do you think are the main benefits of globalisation?
  • Are there any disadvantages?
  • Do you think cultural differences are being lost as the world becomes more connected?

Globalisation has reshaped human society in ways that are both genuinely beneficial and deeply challenging. At its best, it has lifted hundreds of millions of people out of poverty, connected cultures, and made possible scientific and technological collaborations that would have been unimaginable to previous generations.

First of all, the economic benefits are real. Global trade allows countries to specialise in what they do best, making goods more affordable and creating economic growth across borders. The flow of ideas across cultures has accelerated innovation in medicine, technology, and the arts. Many of the things we value most in daily life — from the food we eat to the devices we use — exist because of global supply chains.

At the same time, globalisation has created serious challenges. Economic gains have not been distributed evenly, and many communities have experienced real losses as manufacturing moved to lower-cost countries. Cultural homogenisation is another concern — as global brands and media platforms spread, distinctive local cultures face pressure to survive. To sum up, I think globalisation is neither something to celebrate uncritically nor to reverse — the challenge is to design the international rules and institutions that maximise its benefits while protecting those most vulnerable to its disruptions.

Monologue tips: Organise into: introduction → 2–3 main points → conclusion. Use connectors: "First of all…", "Another important point is…", "To sum up…". Aim for 2 full minutes — don't rush. Examiners reward range and fluency, not perfection.
