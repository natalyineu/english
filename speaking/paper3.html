Speaking — Practice Paper 3

50 marks · 13 minutes total · 4 parts · Topic: Travel & the Natural World

How to use this page: Practise with a partner (one plays the interlocutor, one the candidate), or record yourself speaking. Click any question to highlight it. Aim for natural, fluent responses — not memorised scripts.
Part 1 Personal Questions 3 minutes
3 min The interlocutor asks your name, spelling, country of origin, then chooses up to 5 questions — one from each topic below.

The interlocutor starts: "Hello. My name's [name]. Can you spell your surname for me, please?" then "Where are you from?" — then selects one question from each topic.

Travel
  • ▸Have you travelled to any other countries?
  • ▸What do you think is the best way to travel — by plane, train or car?
  • ▸What kind of holiday do you most enjoy?
Q: Have you travelled to any other countries?

"Yes, I've been lucky enough to visit several. One trip that really stands out was to Portugal — I was struck by how different the everyday pace of life felt there. I genuinely believe that travelling is one of the best ways to broaden your perspective and challenge assumptions you didn't even know you had. That said, I also think you don't need to go far — exploring your own country properly can be just as eye-opening."
Nature
  • ▸Do you spend much time outdoors?
  • ▸What is your favourite season and why?
  • ▸Are you concerned about environmental issues?
Q: Do you spend much time outdoors?

"I try to, yes — I find that even a short walk in a park makes a noticeable difference to how I feel. There's something about being in natural surroundings that gives you a sense of perspective which is hard to get indoors. What I've been doing lately is cycling to places rather than driving, which is a small change but it means I'm outdoors far more than before."
Food
  • ▸What kind of food do you enjoy most?
  • ▸Can you cook? What dishes can you make?
  • ▸Have you ever tried food from another culture that you really liked?
Q: What kind of food do you enjoy most?

"My tastes are fairly eclectic, to be honest. I love food that's fresh and full of flavour — Mediterranean cooking is probably my favourite, particularly anything with good olive oil and herbs. What I've come to appreciate more recently is how food connects people — a shared meal is one of the most straightforward ways to bring people together, and I think that social aspect is just as important as the taste."
Sport
  • ▸Do you play any sports?
  • ▸Do you prefer watching sport or playing it?
  • ▸What do you think are the benefits of regular exercise?
Q: Do you play any sports?

"I play tennis fairly regularly — I started when I was young and I've kept it up because it's a great combination of fitness and social activity. What I particularly enjoy about a racket sport is the competitive element — it genuinely pushes you to improve each time. I think sport teaches you a lot about handling both success and disappointment, which are skills that transfer to many other areas of life."
Weather
  • ▸What is the weather like in your country?
  • ▸Does the weather affect your mood?
  • ▸Would you prefer to live in a hot or cold climate?
Q: What is the weather like in your country?

"The seasons are quite pronounced — cold winters and warm summers, so there's a real contrast throughout the year. I actually quite like that contrast, though winters can be challenging. What I find is that people here have adapted well — you develop a kind of resilience to the cold and genuinely appreciate the first warm spring day in a way that people in warmer climates perhaps don't."
Part 2 Role-plays 3 minutes
3 min Two role-play scenarios. In Scenario 1 Candidate A initiates; in Scenario 2 Candidate B initiates.
Scenario 1 — A initiates
Planning a holiday together
Candidate A: you want to go somewhere adventurous, like hiking in the mountains.
Candidate B: you prefer a relaxing beach holiday.
"I've been thinking about where we should go this summer — I'd love to try something different this year."
Scenario 2 — B initiates
B starts Tourist information office
Candidate B: you are a tourist looking for recommendations for a 3-day visit to the city.
Candidate A: you work at the tourist office.
"Excuse me, I've just arrived and I have no idea where to start — can you help me?"
Part 3 Discussion 4 minutes
4 min Discuss the points on your task sheet together and reach decisions. You have 20 seconds to read before starting.

Task sheet:

How can our town become more environmentally friendly?
Situation: Your town council is deciding how to improve the local environment. Discuss the options below and decide which would make the biggest difference.
▸Creating more cycle lanes
▸Reducing car traffic in the town centre
▸Planting more trees and green spaces
▸Introducing recycling incentives for residents
Useful language: "I think we should…" · "What about…?" · "I'd prefer… because…" · "Could we compromise on…?" · "That's a good point, but…" · "Let's decide on…"
Part 4 Monologue 3 minutes
3 min 30 seconds to make notes → speak for up to 2 minutes → follow-up questions from interlocutor.

The interlocutor gives you one of the topic cards below. You have 30 seconds to prepare, then speak for up to 2 minutes. The interlocutor will then ask follow-up questions.

Card 1
A place you would love to visit
Follow-up questions
  • Where would you most like to go and why?
  • What would you do there?
  • What do you think travel teaches us about the world?

There are many places I dream of visiting, but if I had to choose one, it would be Japan. The contrast between ancient tradition and cutting-edge modernity fascinates me enormously. On one hand, you have centuries-old temples, tea ceremonies, and carefully preserved cultural practices; on the other, you have Tokyo — one of the most technologically advanced cities on earth.

What draws me most is the concept of "wabi-sabi" — the Japanese aesthetic of finding beauty in imperfection and transience. I would love to experience the cherry blossom season, walk through bamboo forests in Kyoto, and simply observe how Japanese society functions with such remarkable efficiency and courtesy.

Another reason is the food. Japanese cuisine is extraordinarily diverse — from the simplicity of soba noodles to the precision of sushi — and experiencing it in its home context would be something truly special. To sum up, Japan represents for me the ideal travel destination because it would challenge my assumptions, expand my cultural understanding, and offer beauty at every turn.

Card 2
The importance of protecting the natural world
Follow-up questions
  • What environmental problems concern you most?
  • What can individuals do to help?
  • Do you think governments do enough to protect the environment?

Protecting the natural world is not simply a matter of preserving beautiful landscapes — it is a question of survival. Ecosystems provide services that human life depends on entirely: clean air, fresh water, stable climate, and food production. When we destroy habitats, we do not just lose biodiversity; we undermine the very systems that sustain us.

First of all, consider the scale of what we are losing. Scientists estimate that species are becoming extinct at a rate one thousand times higher than the natural background rate. Many of these species have not even been discovered, let alone studied for their potential medical or scientific value. Once they are gone, they are gone forever.

Secondly, protecting nature is also an economic argument. Tourism, agriculture, fisheries — these are trillion-dollar industries that depend on healthy ecosystems. Short-term profits from deforestation or overfishing are vastly outweighed by the long-term costs. To sum up, I believe protecting the natural world should be treated not as an optional luxury, but as the most urgent priority of our time — because without a functioning biosphere, nothing else we value is possible.

Monologue tips: Organise into: introduction → 2–3 main points → conclusion. Use connectors: "First of all…", "Another important point is…", "To sum up…". Aim for 2 full minutes — don't rush. Examiners reward range and fluency, not perfection.
