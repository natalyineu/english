📖 Reading Strategies

🔍 Part 1 — Multiple Choice ▶
1️⃣Read the questions first (not the options), then read the passage. Questions guide your focus.
✂️Eliminate distractors. Two options are usually plausible — the correct one is always directly supported by the text.
🔗Locate the evidence. Underline the exact line in the passage that proves your answer. Never guess.
⚠️Watch for paraphrasing. The text rarely uses the same words as the question — look for synonyms and re-worded ideas.
🚫Avoid "sounds right" traps. Options that include vocabulary from the text are often wrong — check the meaning, not the words.
🧩 Part 2 — Sentence Gap-fill ▶
👀Read the whole text first without filling any gaps to understand the overall meaning.
🔄Read all sentence options (A–G) before choosing. The extra sentence is a deliberate distractor.
🔗Check cohesion: pronouns (this/that/they), linkers (however/therefore), and topic continuity must all match around the gap.
✏️Fill easiest gaps first. Cross out used options as you go — this simplifies later choices.
✅Re-read when done with all gaps filled to check the text flows logically.
🗂️ Part 3 — Matching ▶
⚡Skim all short texts first (30 seconds each) to grasp the main subject of each section.
🎯Read each question and identify 1–2 key concepts to locate in a text — paraphrase in your head.
🔢Note text letters. If a text matches multiple questions, that is allowed — each text can be used more than once.
🚫Avoid topic matching. Match meaning, not just the same vocabulary — closely related synonyms signal the right text.
✔️ Part 4 — True / False / Not Given ▶
📌True = the text directly confirms the statement. False = the text directly contradicts it. Not Given = the text doesn't mention it at all.
🔍Locate before you judge. Find the relevant sentence in the text; don't decide from memory.
⚠️"Not Given" does NOT mean False. If the text is simply silent on the point, it's Not Given — even if the statement seems unlikely.
🧠Don't use outside knowledge. Base every answer only on what the text says, even if you know otherwise.

✍️ Writing Strategies

📧 Task 1 — Email / Letter (150–200 words) ▶
📋Identify all bullet points in the task. Each must be addressed — missing one loses marks.
👤Match register to audience. A formal email to a company director ≠ an informal email to a friend. Check the task instruction.
📐Structure: Opening greeting → reason for writing → body (one paragraph per bullet) → closing → sign-off.
🎨Add a personal touch. Examiners reward relevant detail — don't just list; explain, react, or give a reason.
✂️Count words before submitting. Under 130 = automatic mark penalty. Over 230 = time wasted and coherence often drops.
📝 Task 2 — Essay / Article / Report (180–220 words) ▶
⏱️Spend 5 minutes planning. Write a brief outline (intro, 2–3 body points, conclusion) before you start — it prevents mid-essay panic.
📐Essay structure: Introduction (restate the topic) → Point 1 with example → Point 2 with example → Conclusion (summary + opinion).
🔗Use discourse markers: Firstly / Furthermore / On the other hand / In conclusion. These lift your coherence score.
🎓Aim for B2 vocabulary: Show range — avoid repeating the same words. Use collocations (make a decision, have an impact).
🔍Proofread for grammar. Tense consistency, subject-verb agreement, and article use (a/an/the) are the top error sources at B2.
💡 Writing Assessment Criteria ▶
📊Content (40%): All task requirements addressed, relevant ideas, appropriate level of detail.
📊Organisation (20%): Clear paragraphing, logical flow, appropriate opening and closing.
📊Language (40%): Range of vocabulary and grammar, accuracy, register consistency.
💬Safe vs. ambitious: A sentence with complex grammar and a small error is usually better than a simple sentence with no errors — examiners reward range.

🎧 Listening Strategies

👂 Before & During the Recording ▶
⚡Use preview time. Read questions before the audio starts — underline key words so you know what to listen for.
🎯Listen for the answer concept, not exact words. Speakers often paraphrase — the answer idea comes in different words.
✏️Note key words as you listen (numbers, names, opinions). You won't remember everything — jot it down.
🔄If you miss an answer, move on. Don't dwell — staying on a missed question causes you to miss the next one too.
🧩 Question Types ▶
🔢Part 1 (Monologue MC): Predict the topic from questions. The answer is stated explicitly, not implied.
📝Part 2 (Gap-fill): Answers are often single words or short phrases. Spelling must be correct.
🗣️Part 3 (Dialogue MC): Listen for agreement/disagreement, opinions, and attitudes — not just facts.
✔️Part 4 (True/False): The recording will confirm or contradict each statement directly — wait for clear evidence.

🗣️ Speaking Strategies

🗨️ General Speaking Tips ▶
🎯Keep talking. Silence loses marks. If you don't know a word, describe it: "the thing you use to…", "a kind of…".
🔗Link your ideas with connectors: and, but, however, because, which means that. This shows fluency.
🔄Self-correct out loud. Correcting yourself mid-sentence is fine — it shows grammatical awareness.
🎓Use B2 vocabulary. Instead of "good", say "beneficial / effective / significant". Examiners notice lexical range.
👂React to your partner. In paired tasks, ask a follow-up or acknowledge their point — "That's a good point. I also think…"
📊 Speaking Assessment Criteria ▶
🎤Fluency & Coherence: Speak without long pauses; connect ideas logically. Hesitations with "um/well" are natural — silence is not.
📚Lexical Resource: Use precise, varied vocabulary. Paraphrase when you don't know a word.
🏗️Grammatical Range & Accuracy: Mix simple and complex sentences. Occasional errors are acceptable if communication is clear.
🔊Pronunciation: Be clear and intelligible — perfect accent is not required, but word stress and sentence rhythm matter.
🤝Interactive Communication: Listen to your partner, build on their ideas, manage turns politely.
🛠️ Useful Phrases ▶
🎯Giving opinions: From my perspective… / In my view… / I strongly believe that… / It seems to me that…
🔄Agreeing: That's a very valid point. / I couldn't agree more. / Absolutely — and I'd add that…
↔️Disagreeing politely: I see your point, but… / I'm not entirely convinced because… / With respect, I think…
⏸️Buying time: That's an interesting question. Let me think for a moment… / Well, it depends on…
📝Summarising: So to sum up… / All things considered… / Ultimately, I think…

✅ Exam Day Checklist

📅 What to do on Exam Day ▶
🌅Day before: No cramming. Light review of your weakest topic for 30 minutes max, then rest.
🍳Morning of: Eat well, arrive 20 minutes early. Nerves are normal — shallow breathing raises them; slow breathing lowers them.
📖Reading & Writing paper: Allocate 65 min per section; move on when your time is up even if you're not satisfied.
🎧Listening paper: Read questions in every pause. Don't panic over a missed answer — leave it and keep listening.
🗣️Speaking exam: Warm up by speaking English for 5 minutes beforehand (to a friend, or out loud to yourself).
🚫Never leave blanks. A guess scores 25–33% on multiple choice. A blank scores 0%.
