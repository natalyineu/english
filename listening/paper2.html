Part 3 Gap-fill — Digital Detox 6 marks

Read the transcript of a podcast interview. Then complete the sentences below. Write one or two words from the transcript for each gap.

🎧 In the real exam, you hear this. Here you can read the transcript to practise.
Podcast transcript
Host: "Welcome to Mind Matters. Today I'm talking with wellness consultant James Ford about digital detox — switching off from technology. James, how did you first get interested in this?"

James: "I was working sixty-hour weeks and spending every spare minute on my phone. Then one weekend I left it at home by accident and had the best Saturday I'd had in years. That was my turning point."

Host: "And what exactly does a digital detox involve?"

James: "At its simplest, it means choosing specific periods — anything from a few hours to several weeks — when you deliberately avoid using digital devices. No phone, no laptop, no tablet."

Host: "What are the main benefits people report?"

James: "Better sleep is the most common thing people mention. Many also report significant improvements in their concentration and a reduction in anxiety levels."

Host: "Is it difficult to stick to?"

James: "The first twenty-four hours are genuinely hard for most people — there's real withdrawal. But by day three, the majority say they feel calmer and more present."

Host: "Any practical tips for listeners wanting to try it?"

James: "Start small — try a single evening without your phone. Tell your friends and family in advance so they don't worry. And crucially, plan something enjoyable for the time you free up, otherwise you'll just sit there feeling restless."

5. By day ___, most people feel calmer and more present.
6. James advises planning something ___ to fill the time you free up.
Part 4 Multiple Choice — History of the Internet 7 marks

Read the transcript of a radio talk, then choose the correct answer for each question.

🎧 In the real exam, you hear this passage. Here you can read the transcript to practise.
Radio talk transcript

The internet has transformed human society more profoundly than almost any invention in history. Yet its origins were remarkably modest. In 1969, a small network called ARPANET connected just four universities in the United States. Its original purpose was not to help people communicate socially but to allow the military and academic researchers to share data efficiently and to maintain communication networks even if part of the system was destroyed.

The invention that truly opened the internet to the public was the World Wide Web, created in 1989 by British scientist Tim Berners-Lee while working in Switzerland. The Web allowed ordinary people to publish and access information using a simple system of linked pages — what we now call websites. Within five years of its launch, there were over ten thousand websites. Within a decade, that number had grown to tens of millions.

The arrival of broadband internet in the early 2000s changed everything again. For the first time, people could stream video, download music quickly, and use complex web applications. This paved the way for the modern streaming services, social media platforms, and cloud computing systems we rely on today.

Social media in particular has had a profound impact on how people communicate. Platforms such as Facebook, launched in 2004, gave individuals the ability to build large social networks online. By 2023, over five billion people worldwide were using the internet regularly — more than sixty percent of the world's population.

Critics, however, point to serious negative consequences. The spread of misinformation has accelerated dramatically. Online platforms can amplify false information far more quickly than it can be corrected. Psychologists also raise concerns about the effects of social media on young people's mental health, particularly around body image and social comparison. Governments around the world are now grappling with how to regulate an environment that no single country controls.

1. What was the original purpose of ARPANET?

2. What did Tim Berners-Lee create in 1989?

3. How many websites existed within five years of the Web's launch?

4. What did broadband internet make possible for the first time?

5. How many people used the internet regularly by 2023?

6. What does the speaker say about misinformation online?

7. What challenge do governments face regarding the internet?
