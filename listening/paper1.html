Conversation 1 — Airport
Man: "Excuse me — is this the right gate for the Manchester flight?"
Woman: "No, that flight leaves from gate twelve. This is gate eight — it's the Edinburgh service."
Man: "Oh no. Is gate twelve far from here?"
Woman: "Just turn left and follow the signs. You should be fine if you hurry."

1. What does the man need to do?

Conversation 2 — Restaurant
Woman: "I'm afraid your table won't be ready for another twenty minutes."
Man: "That's fine. Could we wait at the bar?"
Woman: "Of course. I'll come and find you when your table is ready."
Man: "Could we take a menu with us to look at while we wait?"
Woman: "Absolutely. Here you are."

2. What will the man do while he waits?

Conversation 3 — Office
Woman: "Has the new IT system been set up yet?"
Man: "It was supposed to be ready yesterday, but the technicians have run into some compatibility problems."
Woman: "So when do they think it'll be working?"
Man: "They're hoping to have it sorted by Friday at the latest."

3. Why is the IT system not ready?

Conversation 4 — Phone Call
Man: "Hello, I ordered a parcel online last week and it still hasn't arrived."
Woman: "Could I take your order number, please?"
Man: "Yes, it's BX four seven nine two."
Woman: "Let me check… I can see there's been a delay at our distribution centre. It should reach you by Thursday."
Man: "Thursday? It was meant to arrive last Monday."

4. What does the man find out?

Conversation 5 — Shop
Woman: "Excuse me, I bought this lamp here last month and it's stopped working."
Man: "I'm sorry to hear that. Do you have your receipt?"
Woman: "Yes, here it is."
Man: "That's fine. We can exchange it for a new one or offer you a full refund — whichever you prefer."
Woman: "I'd like a replacement, please."

5. What does the shop offer the woman?

Conversation 6 — Doctor's Surgery
Woman: "I've been getting headaches almost every day this week."
Doctor: "Are you drinking enough water? Dehydration is one of the most common causes of regular headaches."
Woman: "Probably not as much as I should."
Doctor: "Try drinking at least two litres a day and see if that helps. Come back if it doesn't improve within a week."

6. What does the doctor advise the woman to do?

Conversation 7 — Planning a Trip
Man: "I think we should book a camping trip for the bank holiday weekend."
Woman: "Camping? I'd really rather have a proper bathroom, to be honest."
Man: "Fair enough. What about a holiday cottage? We'd still be in the countryside."
Woman: "Yes, that's a much better idea. We could look online tonight."

7. What do the friends agree to do?

Part 3 Gap-fill — Urban Gardening 6 marks

Read the transcript of a radio interview. Then complete the sentences below. Write one or two words from the transcript for each gap.

🎧 In the real exam, you hear this. Here you can read the transcript to practise.
Radio interview transcript
Presenter: "Welcome to City Talk. Today I'm joined by Dr Anita Patel, who runs the Urban Roots community gardening project. Dr Patel, how long has the project been running?"

Dr Patel: "We launched in 2019, so this is our fifth year. We've seen incredible growth — far beyond what we expected."

Presenter: "And what's the project's main goal?"

Dr Patel: "Our aim is to give people living in cities access to fresh, affordable vegetables. We focus mainly on low-income neighbourhoods where supermarkets are far away or prices are very high."

Presenter: "How many growing sites do you currently have?"

Dr Patel: "We now have thirty-two sites across the city. Some are small — just a few raised beds on a rooftop — but others are quite extensive."

Presenter: "What do volunteers actually do when they come?"

Dr Patel: "They plant seeds, water the beds, and help with the harvest. We also run fortnightly workshops on composting and natural pest control."

Presenter: "Are there benefits beyond the food itself?"

Dr Patel: "Absolutely. Many participants say they feel less isolated since joining. The mental health improvements have been really noticeable — people form genuine friendships here."

Presenter: "And what does the future hold for Urban Roots?"

Dr Patel: "We've just received a government grant to expand into three new districts before the end of the year. We're very excited."

Complete the sentences using words from the transcript above.

1. Urban Roots has been running for ___ years.
2. The project focuses on areas where ___ are far away or expensive.
3. The project currently runs ___ sites across the city.
4. Volunteers help with planting, watering, and the ___.
5. Participants say they feel less ___ since joining the project.
6. The project received a government ___ to expand into new areas.
Part 4 Multiple Choice — Renewable Energy 7 marks

Read the transcript of a documentary extract, then choose the correct answer for each question.

🎧 In the real exam, you hear this passage. Here you can read the transcript to practise.
Documentary transcript

The race for clean energy is reshaping economies around the world. Of all the technologies involved, solar power has seen the most dramatic transformation. Once dismissed as too expensive for ordinary households, solar panels have fallen in cost by more than eighty percent over the past ten years. Last year, solar accounted for nearly a third of all new electricity generation installed worldwide — more than coal, gas, and nuclear combined.

Wind energy has also expanded rapidly. The United Kingdom now leads the world in offshore wind capacity, with more than ten thousand turbines operating around its coastlines. Unlike solar, wind turbines can generate power throughout the night, though their output depends heavily on wind speeds and weather conditions.

One persistent challenge facing the renewable energy sector is storage. On particularly sunny or windy days, power stations often generate far more electricity than the grid needs. Without adequate storage systems, this surplus is simply wasted. Scientists are therefore racing to develop more efficient battery technology. Some promising new designs can already store energy for up to seventy-two hours — a significant improvement on older systems.

The human impact of this energy transition should not be overlooked. Renewable energy creates many new jobs, particularly in engineering and manufacturing. However, it is also putting thousands of workers in fossil fuel industries out of work. Governments across the world are struggling to manage what analysts call the "just transition" — ensuring that communities built around coal mining and oil extraction do not simply collapse.

Despite all of these challenges, renewable energy now supplies over thirty percent of global electricity, a figure that has doubled in just eight years. Most energy analysts believe the transition away from fossil fuels is now irreversible, though achieving full carbon neutrality by 2050 will require far greater investment than governments are currently planning.

1. What has happened to the cost of solar panels in the past decade?

2. What does the speaker say about solar energy's share of new installations last year?

3. What advantage does wind power have over solar energy?

4. What happens to surplus energy on very sunny or windy days?

5. What progress has been made with new battery technology?

6. What does the "just transition" refer to?

7. What share of global electricity do renewables now supply?
