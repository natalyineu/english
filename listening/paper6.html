Customer: "Oh, that's inconvenient. Can I at least get a refund instead?"

Conversation 2 — Concert Venue
Man: "I've heard great things about the jazz festival next weekend. Have you got tickets?"
Woman: "I looked but the Saturday evening concert is sold out. There are still tickets for Sunday afternoon though."
Man: "Sunday afternoon works. Shall we get two?"
Woman: "Let's do it. I'll book them now before they go too."

2. What do the friends decide?

Conversation 3 — Relationship Discussion
Woman: "I've been offered a job in Dubai. It's a brilliant opportunity but it means a two-year contract abroad."
Man: "Wow. What does your partner think?"
Woman: "He's actually very supportive. He said he can work remotely so he'd come with me."
Man: "That's great — it makes the decision a lot easier, doesn't it?"

3. What does the woman's partner plan to do?

Conversation 4 — Career Advice
Student: "I'm torn between doing a master's degree and starting work straight after graduation."
Advisor: "It depends on the field. In some careers, work experience is more valuable than another qualification."
Student: "I want to go into marketing."
Advisor: "In that case, I'd honestly recommend getting real industry experience first. You can always study later."

4. What does the advisor recommend for a career in marketing?

Conversation 5 — Train Station
Passenger: "Excuse me, when is the next train to Bristol?"
Staff: "There was one at quarter past but it's been delayed. The next one should leave at half past four."
Passenger: "Is that confirmed or might it be delayed too?"
Staff: "It's showing on time at the moment but there are signal problems on the line, so I'd keep an eye on the board."

5. What does the staff member tell the passenger?

Conversation 6 — School Parents' Evening
Teacher: "Your daughter is doing very well overall. Her written work is excellent."
Parent: "That's great to hear. Are there any areas she needs to work on?"
Teacher: "She's a little quiet in class discussions. Encouraging her to speak up more at home might help build her confidence."
Parent: "I'll make sure we discuss things more at dinner."

6. What area does the teacher suggest the girl should develop?

Conversation 7 — Technology Discussion
Man: "Have you seen the new AI assistant everyone's talking about?"
Woman: "I've tried it. It's impressive but sometimes it gives you very confident-sounding wrong answers."
Man: "That's the problem, isn't it? How do you know when to trust it?"
Woman: "You have to fact-check anything important. It's a useful tool but it's not a reliable source on its own."

7. What concern does the woman raise about the AI assistant?

Documentary transcript
Narrator: "Street art has undergone a remarkable transformation over the past three decades. Once dismissed as simple vandalism, it is now exhibited in major galleries and sells at auction for millions of pounds. The artist most responsible for this shift in perception is Banksy, whose identity remains unknown despite decades of international fame."

Art critic, Elena Marsh: "What Banksy understood, and what many street artists since have learned, is that the location of a piece is as important as the image itself. A stencil placed on the wall of a bank or a prison says something that the same image hung in a gallery simply cannot."

Narrator: "Cities around the world have responded to the street art movement in very different ways. Some municipalities have launched official programmes, commissioning artists to decorate walls in deprived neighbourhoods, with the aim of reducing crime and improving community pride."

Urban planner, Raj Sharma: "In one district we surveyed, crime fell by twenty-two percent in the twelve months following a major street art project. Whether that's directly caused by the art or by the wider community investment, it's hard to say — but the correlation is striking."

Narrator: "Not everyone welcomes the trend, however. Some residents feel that commissioned murals imposed from outside lack the authenticity that made street art compelling in the first place — the element of surprise, the risk, the rebellion against authority."

Part 4 Multiple Choice — Social Media and Society 7 marks

Read the transcript of a podcast extract, then choose the correct answer for each question.

🎧 In the real exam, you hear this passage. Here you can read the transcript to practise.
Podcast transcript

Social media has fundamentally changed the way human beings relate to one another, and not always in the ways we anticipated when these platforms first emerged. Proponents argued that connecting billions of people would foster mutual understanding, reduce conflict, and give ordinary individuals a voice equal to that of traditional media. Two decades on, the picture is considerably more complicated.

One of the most discussed effects is the so-called "echo chamber" phenomenon. Social media algorithms are designed to show users content that matches their existing views and preferences, which means people are increasingly exposed only to opinions they already hold. Research suggests this has contributed to rising political polarisation in many countries, as people on different sides of debates have access to almost entirely separate information environments.

The impact on young people's mental health has attracted particularly intense scrutiny. Multiple studies have found correlations between high social media use and increased rates of anxiety and depression among teenagers, particularly girls. Psychologists point to comparison culture — the tendency to measure oneself against idealised images — as a key mechanism. However, some researchers argue that the relationship is more complex than a simple cause and effect, and that pre-existing vulnerabilities may be the more significant factor.

On the positive side, social media has democratised information and provided platforms for marginalised voices. Social movements that might previously have remained local have been able to organise globally. Charitable campaigns have raised hundreds of millions of pounds for causes that would otherwise have received no attention.

Governments are now attempting to regulate the sector, though progress is slow. The central difficulty is that the same features that enable harmful content to spread — speed, scale, algorithmic amplification — are also what makes social media effective for legitimate communication. Finding a balance remains one of the defining policy challenges of the age.

1. What did early supporters of social media believe it would achieve?

2. What causes the "echo chamber" effect described in the text?

3. What group does research suggest is most negatively affected by social media?

4. What do some researchers argue about the link between social media and mental health?

5. What positive effect does the speaker mention?

6. Why is regulating social media difficult, according to the speaker?

7. How does the speaker describe the overall question of regulating social media?
