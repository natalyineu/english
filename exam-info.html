Written Paper — Skill by Skill

🎧 Listening 26 marks · ~30 min

1
Multiple Choice — 7 short conversations
Listen to seven short dialogues (including one formal situation). For each, choose the best answer from 3 options. Tested: gist, purpose, attitude, context.
7 marks
2
Multiple Choice — longer recordings
Two or three longer recordings. Questions test topic, speaker relationships, opinions, and implied meaning.
6 marks
3
Note-taking — write in 1–5 words
Listen and fill in a form or notes with specific information. Answers must be 1–5 words. Spelling counts. Numbers can be written as digits.
7 marks
4
Multiple Choice — discussion or interview
A longer discussion. Questions focus on gist, opinions, cause and effect, and the speakers' views.
6 marks
Exam Tips — Listening
  • Read each question before the recording starts — you get preview time.
  • Part 3 answers are often paraphrased — the exact word from the recording is usually correct.
  • Recordings are played twice — use the first listen for the gist, second for answers.
  • Wrong options often include words you heard — focus on meaning, not keywords.

📖 Reading 26 marks · ~1h 5min

1
Multiple Choice — long text
A long text (news article, review, report, or proposal). Six 4-option questions. Tests detailed comprehension, writer's attitude, implied meaning.
6 marks
2
Gap-fill — choose from sentences A–G
A text with 6 gaps. Seven sentences (A–G) to choose from — one is a distractor you won't use. Tests text cohesion, reference words, logical flow.
6 marks
3
Matching — 4 short texts
Four short texts (A–D) + 7 statements. Match each statement to the correct text. The same text can be used more than once.
7 marks
4
Short answers — write up to 5 words
A narrative or informational text. Answer 7 questions in up to 5 words each. You mark this yourself — model answers are provided.
7 marks
Exam Tips — Reading
  • Part 1: Read the whole text first for gist, then return to questions.
  • Part 2: Look at the words just before and after each gap — reference words (it, this, they, however) are key clues.
  • Part 3: Skim all four texts first. Underline the key idea of each. Then match carefully — wording will be paraphrased.
  • Part 4: Copy words directly from the text when possible. Stay within 5 words — longer answers get no marks.

✍️ Writing 24 marks · ~1h 5min

1
Formal email or letter — 100–150 words
Respond to a prompt intended for a public or professional audience. For example: a letter to a magazine, a reply to a job advertisement, a formal email to an organisation.
12 marks
2
Personal letter, narrative, or descriptive text — 150–200 words
A more personal piece of writing. Could be a letter to a friend, a story, a description of a place or event. Register is semi-formal or informal.
12 marks
How each task is marked (12 marks each)
Content
4 marks
Did you cover all the required points? Is everything relevant to the task?
Organisation
4 marks
Is the text well-structured? Are paragraphs logical? Does it read naturally?
Language
4 marks
Range and accuracy of vocabulary and grammar. Appropriate register for the task.
Exam Tips — Writing
  • Check the word count: under 100 / under 150 = automatic mark deductions.
  • Task 1 register: formal — no contractions, use Dear Sir/Madam, Yours faithfully.
  • Task 2 register: match the prompt — a letter to a friend = informal language is fine.
  • Aim for variety in vocabulary — don't repeat the same word twice in one paragraph.
  • Leave 3–5 min at the end to proofread each task for missing words and spelling.
  • Use discourse markers: However, Furthermore, As a result, In conclusion — they boost Organisation marks.

Speaking Exam — Separate Appointment

🗣️ Speaking 50 marks · ~13 min

Face-to-face or online with a live examiner. Parts are consecutive — no breaks.

1
Introduction — ~2 min
Give your name and spell it. State your country of origin. Answer 5 general questions about yourself (work, studies, hobbies, future plans).
2
Interaction — respond to situations — ~3 min
The examiner describes 2–3 situations. You must respond naturally — ask for information, make suggestions, agree/disagree, give opinions.
3
Collaborative task — ~3 min
You and the examiner discuss a topic using written prompts (e.g. plan a community event). Make decisions, justify choices, reach an agreement.
4
Extended monologue — ~5 min
30 seconds to prepare → 2-minute talk on a given topic → follow-up questions from the examiner. Show your ability to structure and develop ideas independently.
How speaking is assessed (50 marks total)
Grammar & Vocabulary
Range & accuracy
Variety of structures. Correct use. Appropriate word choices. B2-level language throughout.
Discourse Management
Coherence & fluency
Connected ideas. Appropriate length of turns. Natural use of fillers, linking words, and discourse markers.
Pronunciation
Clarity & stress
Intelligible to a listener. Correct word and sentence stress. Accent doesn't need to be native.
Interactive Communication
Responsiveness
Initiating, maintaining, and closing interactions. Responding appropriately. Asking for clarification when needed.
Exam Tips — Speaking
  • Part 1: Don't give one-word answers — expand every answer with a reason or example.
  • Part 2: If you don't understand the situation, ask: "Could you repeat that, please?"
  • Part 3: The examiner wants discussion — disagree politely, offer alternatives, don't just agree with everything.
  • Part 4: Use your 30s preparation to note 3–4 key points. Structure it: introduction → 2–3 points → conclusion.
  • Silence = lost marks. If stuck, use fillers: "That's an interesting question…", "Let me think about that…"
  • Pronunciation tip: stress the most important word in each sentence — it sounds more natural and confident.

📅 Exam Day Checklist

  • ✅Valid photo ID (passport or national ID card)
  • ✅Arrive 15–20 min early
  • ✅Pencils and pens (for paper-based)
  • ✅Water — but no food during the exam
  • ✅For online: stable internet, quiet room, working microphone and camera
  • ❌No phones, dictionaries, or notes allowed
