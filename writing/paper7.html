Writing — Practice Paper 7

Assessment How Writing is Marked 12 marks each

Each writing task is marked out of 12 across four criteria (3 marks each):

Communicative achievement
Does the writing achieve its purpose and reach the intended reader?
Content
Are all the required points addressed fully and appropriately?
Organisation
Is the writing well structured with clear paragraphs and linking?
Language
Is the range of vocabulary and grammar appropriate and accurate?
Part 1 Formal Email / Letter 12 marks
100–150 words

Write an email to apply for the summer language course. Mention:

  • which language you want to study and why
  • your current level
  • what type of learning environment suits you best

GlobalSpeak Language Academy — Summer Courses

Improve your language skills this summer! We offer intensive courses in over 20 languages for all levels. Email us to apply and tell us about yourself and your learning preferences.

Apply at: admissions@globalspeak.edu

0 words
Model answer

Dear Admissions Team,

I am writing to apply for a place on one of your summer language courses. I would like to study Spanish, as I am planning to spend a year working in Latin America and feel that strong language skills would significantly improve both my professional and personal experience there.

My current level is intermediate — I completed a one-year evening course at my local college two years ago and have maintained some self-study since then, though I would benefit greatly from more structured practice.

In terms of learning environment, I work best in small groups of eight to twelve students, where there is enough individual attention from the teacher but also opportunity for conversation practice with classmates.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours sincerely,
[Your name]

Part 2 Descriptive Essay 12 marks
150–200 words

Write an essay for your college magazine entitled 'A place that holds special memories for me'. Describe the place and explain why it is important to you.

0 words
Model answer

Some places have the remarkable ability to transport you instantly to another time the moment you return to them. For me, that place is my grandmother's garden in the village where she has lived for over fifty years.

It is not a grand or unusual garden by any measure — a modest rectangle of land bordered by an old stone wall, with apple trees at the far end and a wooden bench beneath a rose arch. Yet every corner of it is rich with memory.

I spent many summers there as a child, helping my grandmother tend the vegetable beds, picking strawberries in the heat of July and listening to her stories about the village as it had been in her own childhood. The garden smells of lavender and cut grass, and on warm evenings the air is filled with birdsong.

Whenever I visit now, even after years away, the familiar scents and sounds bring back those summer days with a vividness that no photograph could capture. Some places become part of who we are — and this garden is certainly one of those for me.
