Writing — Practice Paper 1

24 marks · ~1 hour 5 minutes · 2 tasks · Write your response, then reveal the model answer

Assessment How Writing is Marked 12 marks each

Each writing task is marked out of 12 across four criteria (3 marks each):

Communicative achievement
Does the writing achieve its purpose and reach the intended reader?
Content
Are all the required points addressed fully and appropriately?
Organisation
Is the writing well structured with clear paragraphs and linking?
Language
Is the range of vocabulary and grammar appropriate and accurate?
Part 1 Formal Email / Letter 12 marks
100–150 words

Write an email to enter the competition advertised in the notice below. Mention:

  • when you use the centre
  • why you want the membership
  • how to improve our facilities

Free Leisure Centre Membership: Competition

If you sometimes use our leisure centre, but you are not a member, why not win a year's free membership? All you have to do is write to us and tell us how we can make your experience unforgettable!

We will post the name of our winner on our page and send you your free membership card!

Write to: Joanneblaire@llc.com

0 words
Model answer

Dear Ms Blair,

I am writing to enter your free membership competition. I visit your leisure centre two or three times a week, mainly to use the swimming pool and the gym. I have been going there for about six months.

I would love to win the free membership because it would save me a considerable amount of money each month, which I could then spend on sports equipment.

In terms of improving the facilities, I would suggest extending the opening hours at weekends to at least ten o'clock in the evening. Many people work during the day and find it difficult to visit before closing time.

I hope you will consider my entry. I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours sincerely,
[Your name]

Part 2 Personal / Narrative / Descriptive 12 marks
150–200 words

Write an essay for your college magazine entitled 'My earliest childhood memories'. Describe your clearest memory and say why you remember it.

0 words
Model answer

When I think back to my earliest years, one memory stands out more vividly than any other: the day my grandfather taught me to ride a bicycle.

I must have been about five or six years old. It was a warm Saturday morning in summer, and we were in the park near my grandmother's house. My grandfather held the back of the saddle as I pedalled frantically, convinced I would fall the moment he let go. When I finally looked back and realised he had released the bike several seconds earlier, the mixture of surprise and pride I felt was unlike anything I had experienced before.

I think I remember this so clearly because it was the first time I had truly achieved something on my own. My grandfather's patience and encouragement on that morning taught me something I have carried through life: that the hardest part of learning anything is simply trusting yourself to try.

That bicycle has long since gone, but the feeling of that morning remains as sharp as ever.
