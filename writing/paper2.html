Writing — Practice Paper 2

24 marks · ~1 hour 5 minutes · 2 tasks · Write your response, then reveal the model answer

Assessment How Writing is Marked 12 marks each

Each writing task is marked out of 12 across four criteria (3 marks each):

Communicative achievement
Does the writing achieve its purpose and reach the intended reader?
Content
Are all the required points addressed fully and appropriately?
Organisation
Is the writing well structured with clear paragraphs and linking?
Language
Is the range of vocabulary and grammar appropriate and accurate?
Part 1 Formal Email / Letter 12 marks
100–150 words

Write an email to enter the competition advertised below. Mention:

  • what type of photos you take
  • where you usually take them
  • one way to improve the competition

Photo of the Year Competition

Do you love taking photographs? Enter our Photo of the Year competition! Simply email us and tell us about your photography and how we can make this competition even better. The best entry wins a professional camera worth £500!

Write to: photos@cityarts.com

0 words
Model answer

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to enter your Photo of the Year competition. I mainly take landscape and nature photographs, particularly of wildlife in its natural environment.

I usually take my photographs in the countryside near my home, especially in the early morning when the light is best. I also enjoy photographing urban scenes and the contrast between old and modern architecture.

In terms of improving the competition, I would suggest adding a category specifically for young photographers under eighteen. This would encourage more young people to develop their creativity and perhaps discover a lifelong passion for photography.

I hope you will consider my entry and I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours faithfully,
[Your name]

Part 2 Personal Essay 12 marks
150–200 words

Write an essay for your college magazine entitled 'A time when I felt very proud'. Describe what happened and explain why it was so important to you.

0 words
Model answer

There have been several moments in my life when I have felt proud of an achievement, but one particular occasion stands out above all others: the day I completed my first half-marathon.

I had begun running only eight months before the race, initially struggling to complete even short distances without stopping. My training involved early morning runs through the park in all weathers, gradually building up the distance week by week.

On the day of the race, the atmosphere was electric. Thousands of runners lined up together, each with their own reasons for being there. The course took us through the city centre and along the riverside, and as I crossed the finish line after two hours and fourteen minutes, I felt an overwhelming rush of emotion.

What made this achievement so significant was not the time I ran, but the personal challenge it represented. I had set myself a goal that once seemed impossible and had proved to myself that determination and effort can take you further than you ever imagined.
