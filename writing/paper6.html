Writing — Practice Paper 6

24 marks · ~1 hour 5 minutes · 2 tasks · Write your response, then reveal the model answer

Assessment How Writing is Marked 12 marks each

Each writing task is marked out of 12 across four criteria (3 marks each):

Communicative achievement
Does the writing achieve its purpose and reach the intended reader?
Content
Are all the required points addressed fully and appropriately?
Organisation
Is the writing well structured with clear paragraphs and linking?
Language
Is the range of vocabulary and grammar appropriate and accurate?
Part 1 Formal Letter 12 marks
100–150 words

Write a letter to give feedback to the hotel manager. Mention:

  • when you stayed and what you liked
  • one thing that could be improved
  • a suggestion for a new facility

Golden Stay Hotel — Guest Feedback

We value every guest's opinion! Write to our manager and tell us about your stay and how we could make your experience even more enjoyable. All feedback is read personally by our management team.

Write to: manager@goldenstayhotel.com

0 words
Model answer

Dear Manager,

I am writing to share my feedback following my stay at your hotel last month. I spent three nights in one of your superior rooms, and overall I was very impressed with the standard of cleanliness and the friendly, professional attitude of your staff.

The breakfast buffet was particularly enjoyable, offering a wide variety of fresh and well-prepared options. However, I noticed that the gym facilities close at eight in the evening, which is inconvenient for guests who prefer to exercise after dinner.

I would suggest extending the gym hours until ten o'clock at night, and also adding a small relaxation area with comfortable seating where guests could read or work quietly in the evenings.

I would certainly consider returning to your hotel in the future.

Yours sincerely,
[Your name]

Part 2 Reflective Essay 12 marks
150–200 words

Write an essay for your travel magazine entitled 'What travelling has taught me'. Describe an experience and explain what you learned from it.

0 words
Model answer

Travelling has taught me many things, but perhaps the most valuable lesson came from an unexpected experience during a trip to Southeast Asia three years ago.

I had been planning to spend two weeks travelling between major tourist destinations, following a carefully organised itinerary. However, after missing a connecting bus in a small rural town, I found myself stranded without any internet connection and with very little of the local language.

What followed was one of the most rewarding experiences of my life. Local residents, despite the language barrier, went out of their way to help me find accommodation and transport. A family invited me for dinner and showed me extraordinary kindness.

That experience taught me that the most memorable moments of travel rarely appear in guidebooks. By stepping outside the comfort of planned tourism, I discovered something far more valuable than any landmark: the essential goodness of strangers and the ability to connect with people across all cultural boundaries.

Travelling, at its best, teaches us more about humanity than any classroom ever could.
