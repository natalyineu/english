Writing — Practice Paper 3

24 marks · ~1 hour 5 minutes · 2 tasks · Write your response, then reveal the model answer

Assessment How Writing is Marked 12 marks each

Each writing task is marked out of 12 across four criteria (3 marks each):

Communicative achievement
Does the writing achieve its purpose and reach the intended reader?
Content
Are all the required points addressed fully and appropriately?
Organisation
Is the writing well structured with clear paragraphs and linking?
Language
Is the range of vocabulary and grammar appropriate and accurate?
Part 1 Formal Email / Letter 12 marks
100–150 words

Write an email to tell the sports club about your experience. Mention:

  • which sport you enjoy most
  • why you enjoy it
  • one way to improve the facilities

City Sports Club — Member Survey

We want to hear from our members! Tell us about the sport you love and how we can improve our facilities. The most helpful response wins three months of free membership.

Email your reply to: feedback@citysportsclub.com

0 words
Model answer

Dear City Sports Club,

I am writing in response to your member survey. The sport I enjoy most at the club is swimming, which I have practised since childhood. I visit the pool at least three times a week and find it both relaxing and excellent exercise.

What I particularly enjoy about swimming is that it works every part of the body without putting pressure on the joints. I also appreciate the sense of calm it gives me after a busy working day.

With regard to improving the facilities, I would suggest extending the opening hours for the outdoor pool during the summer months. Many members work standard office hours and would benefit from being able to swim in the evenings until nine o'clock.

I hope you find my feedback useful.

Yours sincerely,
[Your name]

Part 2 Opinion Essay 12 marks
150–200 words

Write an essay for your college magazine entitled 'Technology in everyday life — does it always make things better?' Give your opinion with reasons and examples.

0 words
Model answer

There is no doubt that technology has transformed almost every aspect of our daily lives. From smartphones to smart homes, digital tools have made many tasks quicker, easier and more convenient than ever before. However, the question of whether these changes are always positive deserves careful consideration.

On the one hand, technology has brought enormous benefits. Medical advances have saved countless lives, communication tools allow us to connect with people anywhere in the world instantly, and online learning has opened education to millions who might otherwise have been unable to access it.

On the other hand, there are genuine concerns about technology's impact on human wellbeing. Many people find it increasingly difficult to disconnect from their devices, leading to sleep problems and reduced concentration. Social media, in particular, has been linked to anxiety and low self-esteem among young people.

In my opinion, technology itself is neither good nor bad — it is how we choose to use it that matters. With greater awareness and self-discipline, we can enjoy its benefits while minimising its drawbacks.
